# Prior-art boundary

- Cullimore et al. (arXiv:1107.4034) already show that minimum gap alone does
  not determine finite-time adiabatic success.  That observation is not new.
- Bombieri et al. (arXiv:2411.04645; PRX Quantum 6, 020306) show that Rydberg
  graph encodings can introduce localisation and exponentially bad gaps, and
  propose quantum-aware encoding modifications.  Quantum-aware graph
  transformation is not new.
- Hsieh and Sels (arXiv:2512.04494) combine counterdiabatic Rydberg MIS driving
  with subspace reduction.  Neither subspace-reduced gauge potentials nor
  counterdiabatic MIS control can be claimed here.
- Karni, Cohen, and Pick (arXiv:2605.16944) use degree-aware local controls to
  accelerate Rydberg MIS.  Leaf/degree-aware pulse engineering is prior art.
- The closed preceding cycle already falsified static whole-path
  ground-projector preservation for ordinary leaf kernelization.

The only live gap is a graph-theoretic separation specific to exact classical
kernelization: constant vertex deletion, bijective endpoint lift, nearly
unchanged minimum gap, and growing finite-time distortion attributable to path
geometry.  Even this is killed if it reduces to an independent-product effect.
