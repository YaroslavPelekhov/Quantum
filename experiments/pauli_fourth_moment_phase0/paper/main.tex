\begin{abstract}
For Pauli observables with a prescribed anticommutation graph, weighted
sums of squared expectations need not equal the corresponding weighted
independence number. Our main analytic result proves equality for every
line graph and every nonnegative weight vector. The proof maps a line-graph
Hamiltonian to a Majorana bilinear and shows that squared entries of a
skew polar factor satisfy Edmonds' full matching-polytope description.
It yields the sharp inequality
$\norm{A}_*^2/4\leq\nu(R,w)\sum_e A_e^2/w_e$ and reduces the complete
weighted uncertainty problem to maximum-weight matching. This strictly
extends the previously sufficient $h$-perfect route: the infinite family
$L(K_{2k+1})$, $k\geq2$, is not $h$-perfect but is covered by our theorem.
We also give computer-assisted proofs of equality for an explicit family
of simplicial claw-free graphs of order $2m+8$, for every $m\geq0$ and
every nonnegative weight vector. A signed rectangular Gram
identity controls one full-support weighted inequality; an exact
eight-vertex core and a uniform transportation argument supply the
remaining weights. A related three-row identity proves a twelve-vertex
case and yields all-weight corollaries for a published XX-strip and one
specified closure. We then isolate a twenty-four-vertex composition with
a full-support classical facet of bound six. A rational two-copy dual
certificate now proves its quantum maximum is exactly six; all 16384
dual coordinates and 8256 symmetric-support inequalities are checked
with integer arithmetic. A rational feasible point of the first-moment
relaxation has objective $6.50657958$. The classical facet has known
gear/lifting ancestry, so its polyhedral construction is not claimed new.
Exact algebraic and
polyhedral acceptance is separated from numerical discovery. The earlier
archived bundle passes 127 tests and checks 64 artifact hashes; the new
line-graph audit adds 1245 finite root-graph stress cases, 47 direct dense
Majorana checks, and five corruption-controlled tests. The results do not establish weighted
perfection of all simplicial claw-free graphs or a general quantum
strip-composition rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The anticommutation graph records a useful part of the structure of a
many-qubit Pauli Hamiltonian without specifying a particular tensor-product
representation. For observables $P_i=P_i^\dagger$, $P_i^2=I$, place an edge
between $i,j$ exactly when $P_iP_j=-P_jP_i$; nonedges require commutation.
For a graph $G$ and weights $w\geq0$, consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 \beta(G,w)=\sup_{\{P_i\},\rho}\sum_{i\in V(G)}w_i\ev{P_i}^2,
 \qquad \alpha(G,w)=\max_{S\text{ stable in }G}\sum_{i\in S}w_i.
\end{equation}
Here the supremum includes Pauli realizations with exactly the stated
pairwise relations. All upper bounds in this paper apply to every such
realization and every density matrix. A common eigenstate of the commuting
operators in a maximum-weight stable set proves $\beta(G,w)\geq\alpha(G,w)$.

The weighted beta number, the equality termed $\hbar$-perfection, and
facet-based reductions are established concepts \cite{xu}. The
free-fermion solution for simplicial claw-free (SCF) frustration graphs
provides our spectral input \cite{cem}. Neither that solution nor the
unweighted bound by itself resolves the weighted problem: a stable-set
polytope can have non-rank facets. The contribution examined here is a
specific route from coherent, sign-dependent transfer coefficients to
all-weight bounds on explicitly defined graphs.

The paper has three different kinds of outcome. Section~\ref{sec:line}
gives an analytic arbitrary-size theorem. Sections~\ref{sec:gram}--
\ref{sec:xx} give proved, restricted results. Section~\ref{sec:frontier}
gives an exact fixed-weight quantum certificate for a composed graph,
alongside its classical ancestry and earlier failed numerical attempts.
The unresolved question is now a general quantum composition rule, not
the truth of that single inequality. Numerical nonviolations are kept
separate from upper-bound proofs.

The construction uses known classical ideas: stable-set facets, matching,
transportation, and the published XX-strip. We do not claim new graph
decompositions or a new quantum-simulation algorithm. In particular,
solving a constituent atom is not a theorem about arbitrary strip
compositions. Earlier QAOA/MPS performance and symmetry investigations
are outside this manuscript and supply no evidence for its claims.

\section{Preliminaries and rank-to-weight reduction}\label{sec:prelim}
A stable set is a set of pairwise nonadjacent vertices, including the
empty set. Its incidence vector is $\mathbf1_S$, and
$\STAB(G)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_S:S\text{ stable}\}$.
This polytope is full-dimensional and downward closed in the nonnegative
orthant. Thus proving that every squared profile
$x_i=\ev{P_i}^2$ satisfies its complete nonnegative-weight description
proves all-weight equality in \eqref{eq:beta}. Attainability is then
established separately by the commuting stable-set argument.

A graph is claw-free if it has no induced $K_{1,3}$. A nonempty clique
$K$ is simplicial if $N(v)\setminus K$ is a clique for every $v\in K$.
We use SCF componentwise: every connected component is claw-free and
admits such a clique. Induced subgraphs remain SCF componentwise; this
is the inherited structural class used by the free-fermion framework
\cite{cem}. The finite support checks in our certificates also verify
the necessary SCF witnesses directly.

For fixed observables the variational identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variational}
 \max_\rho\sum_iw_i\ev{P_i}^2
 =\max_{\norm{a}_2=1}\norm{\sum_i a_i\sqrt{w_i}P_i}^2
\end{equation}
follows by duality of the Euclidean norm and then maximization over
states. Equivalently, for positive weights the Hamiltonian amplitudes
$b_i=a_i\sqrt{w_i}$ obey $\sum_i b_i^2/w_i=1$.
Zero weights are omitted or obtained by continuity.

\begin{lemma}[SCF rank bound]\label{lem:rank}
For SCF $G$ and every induced support $U$,
$\sum_{i\in U}\ev{P_i}^2\leq\alpha(G[U])$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to work in the universal Pauli algebra and then restrict to
physical representations. Write $A=\sum_i b_iP_i$ and let $Q_k$ be the
sum of $\prod_{i\in S}b_iP_i$ over stable $k$-sets. Within a stable set
the factors commute. For $T(u)=\sum_k(-u)^kQ_k$,
\[
 A^2=\Big(\sum_i b_i^2\Big)I+2Q_2,
 \qquad T(u)T(-u)=I-u^2\Big(\sum_i b_i^2\Big)I+O(u^4).
\]
The SCF sector theorem \cite{cem} gives at most $\alpha(G)$
nonnegative single-particle energies $\nu_j$, with
$T(u)T(-u)=\prod_j(1-u^2\nu_j^2)$ in a sector and Hamiltonian norm at
most $\sum_j\nu_j$. The displayed coefficient implies
$\sum_j\nu_j^2=\sum_i b_i^2$. Cauchy--Schwarz and
\eqref{eq:variational} give the rank bound. Apply it to each component
of the induced support. Degenerate amplitudes follow by continuity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[All weights at independence number two]\label{lem:alpha2}
For any graph with $\alpha(G)=2$,
\[
 \sup_{w\geq0,\,w\ne0}\frac{\beta(G,w)}{\alpha(G,w)}
 =\frac{\beta(G,\mathbf1)}{2}.
\]
Consequently every SCF graph with independence number at most two is
$\hbar$-perfect.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $\alpha(G,w)\leq1$. The weight polytope is
$0\leq w_i\leq1$, $w_i+w_j\leq1$ for every nonedge. Its vertices are
half-integral: a bipartite component of tight equations among fractional
coordinates admits a nonzero alternating perturbation, while an odd
cycle forces every coordinate in its component to be $1/2$.
At an extreme weight, let $H$ have weight one and $L$ weight one-half.
Then $H$ is a clique completely joined to $L$. Cross anticommutation
gives $(A_H+A_L)^2=A_H^2+A_L^2$, and hence the beta bound on a complete
join is the maximum of the bounds on its parts. With
$b=\beta(G,\mathbf1)\geq2$, the extreme-weight bound is at most
$\max(1,b/2)=b/2$. Convexity in $w$ extends it to the weight polytope;
uniform weights attain the ratio. Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} gives $b=2$ in
the SCF case. Cliques are immediate. The half-integral and join tools
are standard; no new polyhedral principle is asserted.
\end{proof}

\section{All line graphs are hbar-perfect}\label{sec:line}
Let $R=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph and $L(R)$ its line graph: the
vertices of $L(R)$ are the edges of $R$, with adjacency when two root
edges share an endpoint. Write $\nu(R,w)$ for the maximum weight of a
matching. Then $\alpha(L(R),w)=\nu(R,w)$.

\begin{lemma}[Skew trace norm controlled by matching]\label{lem:skewmatching}
Let $w_e>0$ and let $A=-A^{\mathsf T}$ be a real matrix supported on
$E(R)$, with upper-triangular entry $A_e$ on edge $e$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:skewmatching}
 \left(\frac{\norm{A}_*}{2}\right)^2
 \leq \nu(R,w)\sum_{e\in E}\frac{A_e^2}{w_e}.
\end{equation}
The constant is sharp for every $R,w$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By nuclear/operator-norm duality, choose a real $Q$ with
$\norm{Q}_{\rm op}\leq1$ and
$\langle A,Q\rangle_F=\norm{A}_*$. Replacing $Q$ by
$(Q-Q^{\mathsf T})/2$ preserves the pairing with skew $A$ and does not
increase the operator norm. Hence take $Q$ skew and put $x_e=Q_e^2$.

For each vertex $v$, the incident sum $\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}x_e$ is at
most the squared norm of row $v$ of $Q$, hence at most one. If
$S\subseteq V$ has odd cardinality, the odd-order skew principal matrix
$Q[S]$ has rank at most $|S|-1$. All its singular values are at most
one, so
\[
 2\sum_{e\in E(S)}x_e\leq\norm{Q[S]}_F^2\leq |S|-1.
\]
Thus $x$ satisfies the nonnegativity, degree and odd-set inequalities
in Edmonds' matching-polytope theorem \cite{edmonds}. It follows that
$\sum_e w_ex_e\leq\nu(R,w)$. Since skew symmetry gives
$\norm{A}_*/2=\sum_e A_eQ_e$, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz proves
\eqref{eq:skewmatching}. For sharpness, support $A$ on a
maximum-weight matching and take $A_e$ proportional to $w_e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Line-graph $\hbar$-perfection]\label{thm:line}
For every finite simple graph $R$ and every nonnegative edge weight $w$,
\[
 \beta(L(R),w)=\alpha(L(R),w)=\nu(R,w).
\]
Consequently every finite line graph is $\hbar$-perfect.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Zero-weight edges may be deleted. Assign a Majorana operator $\gamma_v$
to each root vertex and represent $e=uv$ by the Hermitian involution
$P_e=i\gamma_u\gamma_v$. This is a Pauli realization after a
Jordan--Wigner transform, and its frustration graph is $L(R)$
\cite{cf}. Representation invariance of the weighted beta number
\cite{xu} permits this choice.

For real coefficients $c_e$, let $A$ be the skew matrix with entries
$A_e=c_e$. Orthogonal skew normal form shows
\[
 \norm{\sum_e c_eP_e}=\frac{\norm{A}_*}{2}.
\]
Combining the variational identity \eqref{eq:variational} with
Lemma~\ref{lem:skewmatching}, under
$\sum_e c_e^2/w_e=1$, proves the upper bound $\beta\leq\nu$.
A maximum-weight matching is a stable set of $L(R)$; its Pauli
involutions commute and a joint eigenstate attains $\nu$. Hence equality.
\end{proof}

There is an independent polyhedral derivation. Every line graph is SCF:
it is claw-free, and a root star is a simplicial clique. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} to the line-graph support corresponding to a root star
to obtain the degree inequality. Apply it to the support $E(S)$ induced by
an odd root-vertex set $S$ to obtain
\[
 \sum_{e\in E(S)}\ev{P_e}^2\leq\nu(R[S])\leq (|S|-1)/2.
\]
Together with nonnegativity, these are Edmonds' complete
matching-polytope inequalities. This gives a second proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:line}. The polar argument remains useful because it yields
the sharp coefficient-dependent matrix inequality \eqref{eq:skewmatching}
directly.

\begin{corollary}[Strictly beyond $h$-perfect graphs]\label{cor:strict}
For every $k\geq2$, $L(K_{2k+1})$ is $\hbar$-perfect but not
$h$-perfect.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:line}. For the second, assign
$x_e=1/(2k)$ to every edge of $K_{2k+1}$. Maximal cliques of its line
graph are root stars and triangles; their values are respectively one
and $3/(2k)\leq1$. Every odd cycle of length $\ell\geq5$ has value
$\ell/(2k)\leq(\ell-1)/2$. Thus $x$ satisfies nonnegativity, clique
and odd-hole inequalities. Its total value is $(2k+1)/2$, while every
matching has size at most $k$. It therefore violates the full odd-set
matching inequality by $1/2$, excluding $h$-perfection.
\end{proof}

The theorem supplies an exact polynomial-time uncertainty oracle for all
line-graph Pauli families: compute a maximum-weight matching rather than
simulate a state or solve a noncommutative SDP. It also identifies the
convex mechanism missing from clique and odd-hole bounds: odd principal
skew matrices lose one rank, producing every blossom constraint at once.
The finite C038 campaign verifies the polar-factor inequalities on all
1245 nonempty NetworkX atlas roots, compares the trace-norm formula with
dense Jordan--Wigner matrices in 47 small-root cases, and checks the
strictness witnesses $L(K_5),L(K_7),L(K_9)$ exactly. The largest direct
norm discrepancy is $7.11\times10^{-15}$. These checks guard constants and
indexing; the arbitrary-size conclusion is analytic.

The closest published structures do not by themselves state this result.
Chapman and Flammia identify line-graph Hamiltonians with quadratic
Majorana models \cite{cf}. Zurel, Cohen, and Raussendorf use
$L(K_{2n+1})$ to construct vertices of a Pauli simulation polytope
\cite{zurel}; their objective is magic-state simulation, not the weighted
beta body. McNulty studies measurement-incompatibility robustness on line
graphs and obtains skew-energy bounds \cite{mcnulty}, a different
optimization invariant. Classical work relates skew energy and matching
number for unweighted oriented graphs \cite{tianwong}; consequently the
matrix inequality should not be marketed as the first connection between
these objects. Our priority-sensitive claim is only the exact all-weight
Pauli beta/matching identity and its blossom-polytope proof. The repository
contains the search protocol and unresolved priority risks; external
expert review remains necessary.

\section{A signed Gram bound beyond a single graph}\label{sec:gram}
\subsection{The rectangular family}
For an integer $m\geq0$, define light vertices by occupied cells in
three rows: $A_j=(0,j)$, $B_j=(1,j)$ for $0\leq j\leq m$, together with
$Z=(2,0)$, $U=(0,m+1)$, $V=(1,m+2)$. Two light vertices are adjacent
exactly when they share a row or a column. Add a clique $H_0,H_1,H_c$.
Its light nonneighbors are row zero, row one, and column zero,
respectively. Call the resulting graph $G_m$; its order is $2m+8$.

Every independent triple contains $Z$ and one noncentral vertex from
each other row, in distinct columns. A stable set containing a heavy
vertex has at most one light vertex. Thus $\alpha(G_m)=3$.
The clique $\{A_0,B_0,Z\}$ is simplicial. Claw-freeness can be checked
uniformly: a possible claw involves at most four noncentral column
indices, so it embeds into $G_4$ by renaming those columns. The exact
four-vertex check on $G_4$ is exhaustive. This is a finite-support proof,
not extrapolation from graph sizes.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform full-support bound]\label{thm:full}
For every $m\geq0$, every Pauli realization of $G_m$, and every state,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full}
 \sum_{i\text{ light}}\ev{P_i}^2
 +2\big(\ev{P_{H_0}}^2+\ev{P_{H_1}}^2+\ev{P_{H_c}}^2\big)\leq3.
\end{equation}
Equality is attainable. The associated classical inequality is a facet
of $\STAB(G_m)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Exact transfer coefficients}
Use real amplitudes $a_j,b_j,c,p,q$ on $A_j,B_j,Z,U,V$, and
$r_0,r_1,r_c$ on the heavy vertices. Define
\[
 K_j=-P_{H_0}P_{H_1}P_{A_j}P_{B_j}.
\]
Each $K_j$ is a central Hermitian involution in the full generator
algebra. Commuting a generator through its four factors gives an even
number of sign changes. The square and adjoint are also verified exactly.
In an actual common eigenspace write $K_j=\epsilon_j I$ and form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
 B=\begin{pmatrix}
 a_0&a_1&\cdots&a_m&p&0\\
 \epsilon_0b_0&\epsilon_1b_1&\cdots&\epsilon_mb_m&0&q\\
 c&0&\cdots&0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad M=BB^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
Set $L=\tr M$ and $H=r_0^2+r_1^2+r_c^2$. Sector signs are retained
coherently in this single real matrix. They are not optimized
independently, and we do not assume every abstract sign assignment is a
physical sector.

For $T(u)=I-uQ_1+u^2Q_2-u^3Q_3$, exact expansion in the universal
algebra gives
\begin{align}
 T(u)T(-u)&=I-e_1u^2+e_2u^4-e_3u^6,\label{eq:transfer}\\
 e_1&=L+H,\nonumber\\
 e_2&=e_2(M)+(r_0,r_1)M_{\{0,1\},\{0,1\}}(r_0,r_1)^{\mathsf T}
               +r_c^2\norm{B_{:,0}}^2,\label{eq:e2}\\
 e_3&=\det M.\nonumber
\end{align}
Here $e_2(M)$ is the sum of its principal two-by-two minors. The exact
acceptance algorithm reorders adjacent generators and compares integer
amplitude polynomials, including every zero odd coefficient. The
discovery algorithm instead uses inversion parity.

These finite checks prove the identities for arbitrary $m$. A coefficient
of degree at most four involves at most four noncentral columns. At
degrees five and six, a term comes from a pair and a triple, or two
triples; each triple contains $Z$. At most four other vertices remain.
On the matrix side the degree-six determinant has a factor $c^2$ and
the same bound. Relabel the involved columns into $G_4$, set unused
amplitudes to zero, and transport the word-ordering signs. Thus any
failed coefficient at any size would already fail the exact $G_4$ check.

\subsection{The scalar envelope and the spectral branch}
\begin{lemma}[Three-mode Gram envelope]\label{lem:envelope}
Suppose an SCF Hamiltonian has at most three sector modes and, in every
physical sector, a real positive semidefinite $3\times3$ matrix $M$ with
trace $L$ such that
\[
 e_1=L+H,\qquad e_2\leq e_2(M)+H\lambda_{\max}(M),
 \qquad e_3=\det M.
\]
If $2L+H=1$ and $L,H\geq0$, then the Hamiltonian norm squared is at
most $3/2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $x,y,z\geq0$ be the eigenvalues of $M$, with $y$ largest. Then
$x+y+z=L\leq1/2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 xy+xz+yz+(1-2L)y+2\sqrt{\tfrac32 xyz}
 \leq (\tfrac14+\tfrac L2)^2.
\end{equation}
For fixed $L,y$, the left side increases with $xz$. Replace $x=z=(L-y)/2$
and write $y=s^2/6$. The difference between the right side and the
resulting left side is exactly
\[
 \frac{(s-1)^2(12L+s^2+6s+3)}{48}\geq0.
\]
For the nonnegative sector energies put $S=\nu_1+\nu_2+\nu_3$, padding
zero modes, and define
\[
 f(t)=\left(\frac{t^2-e_1}{2}\right)^2-e_2-2t\sqrt{e_3}.
\]
The symmetric identities in $\nu_j^2$ imply $f(S)=0$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:envelope} yields $f(\sqrt{3/2})\geq0$.
For $t\geq\sqrt{3/2}$,
\[
 f'(t)=t(t^2-e_1)-2\sqrt{e_3}>0,
\]
because $e_1=1-L\leq1$ and $e_3\leq(L/3)^3\leq1/216$.
Consequently $S\leq\sqrt{3/2}$, on the required spectral branch.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:full}]
The two heavy contributions in \eqref{eq:e2} are Rayleigh forms,
bounded together by $H\lambda_{\max}(M)$; use
$\lambda_{\max}(B^{\mathsf T}B)=\lambda_{\max}(M)$ for the column norm.
For half the weights in \eqref{eq:full}, the variational normalization
is $2L+H=1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope} proves the upper bound $3/2$;
rescaling proves \eqref{eq:full}. The commuting pair $\{H_0,U\}$
has total weight three and gives attainment.

For the facet assertion, an affine functional constant on all tight
stable sets must have equal coefficients within each row, by the pairs
$\{H_0,A_j\},\{H_0,U\}$ and $\{H_1,B_j\},\{H_1,V\}$.
The pairs $\{H_c,A_0\},\{H_c,B_0\},\{H_c,Z\}$ equate both row
coefficients and that of $Z$, say to $a$. The triple $\{U,V,Z\}$
sets the right side to $3a$ and every heavy coefficient to $2a$.
Thus the tight affine hull has codimension one.
\end{proof}

\section{All weights on the unbounded family}\label{sec:allweights}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:allweights}
For every $m\geq0$ and every $w\in\R_+^{2m+8}$,
$\beta(G_m,w)=\alpha(G_m,w)$.
\end{theorem}
The argument below combines a fixed exact polyhedral lemma with a uniform
classical refinement. Quantum validity is checked after the complete
classical description is established; no quantum gluing rule is assumed.

For a candidate marginal $x\geq0$, abbreviate
\[
 a=x_{A_0},\quad b=x_{B_0},\quad c=x_Z,\quad
 p=x_{H_0},\quad q=x_{H_1},\quad r=x_{H_c},
\]
\[
 A=x_U+\sum_{j=1}^m x_{A_j},\qquad
 B=x_V+\sum_{j=1}^m x_{B_j},\qquad C=a+b+c+p+q+r.
\]
The use of $A,B$ here denotes scalar marginals, not operators or the
matrix in \eqref{eq:B}. Write $y=(a,b,c,A,B,p,q,r)$.
\begin{proposition}[Uniform classical description]\label{prop:description}
The vector $x$ belongs to $\STAB(G_m)$ if and only if $x\geq0$,
$y\in\STAB(G_0)$, and for $1\leq j\leq m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairs}
 x_{A_j}+x_{B_j}+r\leq1,\qquad
 x_{A_j}+x_{B_j}+C\leq2.
\end{equation}
The positive inequalities describing $\STAB(G_0)$ are in
Table~\ref{tab:core}; coordinate nonnegativity is also required.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Exact core endpoint]\label{lem:endpoint}
Among stable-set distributions of $G_0$ with marginal $y$, the minimum
probability $z$ of selecting both private vertices is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zmin}
 z_{\min}=\max(0,A+B+r-1,A+B+C-2).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lift every one of the 22 stable incidence vectors by the indicator that
both private vertices are selected. The complete nine-dimensional
lifted hull has 24 facets. Its only lower-$z$ facets are the three in
\eqref{eq:zmin}; the others are upper-$z$ or $z$-independent facets.
This finite assertion is certified by full-cube rational clipping and
facet-rank checks, independently of rational double-description discovery.
The exact artifact and acceptance command are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}.

Any feasible decomposition of $y$ supplies a value $z^*$. Lower it to
$z_{\min}$. All lower constraints hold, upper constraints remain valid,
and the point stays in the cube since $0\leq z_{\min}\leq z^*\leq1$.
Completeness of the lifted hull proves feasibility and hence the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:description}]
Collapse the first and second noncentral row cliques to their private
representatives. Stable sets project to stable sets of $G_0$, and
\eqref{eq:pairs} is valid by the indicated clique and rank-two supports.
This proves necessity.

For sufficiency choose the core distribution of Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint}
and let $T=A+B-z_{\min}=\min(A+B,1-r,2-C)$.
Remove the absent/absent mass $1-T$. On the remaining mass construct a
transportation table with row supplies
$(x_{A_1},\ldots,x_{A_m},x_U,T-A)$ and column demands
$(x_{B_1},\ldots,x_{B_m},x_V,T-B)$. The last labels mean absence.
Forbid $(A_j,B_j)$ and the absent/absent cell, allowing every other cell.
Each row forbids at most one column and these forbidden columns are
distinct. Every set of at least two rows therefore sees every column.
The weighted Hall conditions reduce to
$x_{A_j}+x_{B_j}\leq T$ and $(T-A)+(T-B)\leq T$.
The first follows from \eqref{eq:pairs} and nonnegativity, the second
from $z_{\min}\geq0$. Ordinary max-flow/min-cut thus provides a table.

Its joint-present mass is $z_{\min}$ and its single-present masses are
$A-z_{\min},B-z_{\min}$. Condition within each of the four core
presence patterns to choose actual row labels according to the table.
Every label in a row has identical fixed-core neighbors, and forbidden
matching pairs never occur. All outcomes are stable and have marginal
$x$. Zero-mass patterns require no conditioning; $T=0$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:allweights}]
Every positive core row except the full row omits at least one of
$c,A,B$. After expansion its support has independence number at most
two, because every triple of $G_m$ uses $Z$ and both noncentral rows.
The stable bound is unchanged by expansion: projection gives one
direction and choosing the private labels gives the other. Thus
Lemma~\ref{lem:alpha2} proves these expanded rows quantum-mechanically.
It also proves both per-column rows in \eqref{eq:pairs}; their supports
have independence number at most two. Theorem~\ref{thm:full} proves the
full row. Every squared profile therefore satisfies the complete system
of Proposition~\ref{prop:description}, and lies in $\STAB(G_m)$.
This proves every nonnegative weight, with attainment as before.
\end{proof}

\section{A fixed composition theorem and its frontier}\label{sec:frontier}
Take two copies of $X$ with vertices $7,8$ removed. In each copy let
$A=\{1,2,13\}$ and $B=\{4,5,13\}$ in original labels. Join the two
$B$-cliques completely. Add vertices $u,v$ with edge $uv$, join $u$ to
the left $A$-clique and $v$ to the right $A$-clique, and add no other
edges. Call this twenty-four-vertex graph $Y$.

For its zero-based ordering, list the eleven retained original labels
$1,2,3,4,5,6,9,10,11,12,13$ in the left copy, then the right copy, then
$u=22,v=23$. Freeze weights $w_i=1+\mathbf1_{i\in\{6,7\}}$.
The exact graph6 and Pauli realization are archived with this ordering.

\begin{proposition}[Exact classical target]\label{prop:target}
The graph $Y$ is SCF, has $\alpha(Y)=6$, and has 2167 stable sets.
The inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \sum_{i=0}^{23}x_i+x_6+x_7\leq6
\end{equation}
is a full-support facet of $\STAB(Y)$, with 88 tight stable sets of
homogeneous rank 24.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Exact enumeration by recursive graph bitsets agrees with a separate
product construction from all stable sets of the two eleven-vertex
cores and the four connector choices. Both enumerate every stable set,
compute the same maximum six, and agree on all tight sets. Independent
Fraction elimination verifies the homogeneous rank. SCF witnesses are
checked from the graph, including the simplicial clique $\{u,v\}$.
The data and checkers constitute the finite certificate.
\end{proof}
Because the tight affine span has codimension one, \eqref{eq:target}
cannot be a nonnegative linear combination of valid inequalities on
proper vertex supports: each positively weighted summand would have to
be tight on all 88 points and hence define the same full-support
hyperplane. This excludes that particular proof route, not nonlinear
reductions or existing structural theorems.

\begin{theorem}[Exact composed quantum facet, C031]\label{thm:target}
For the fixed graph $Y$ and weights above, $\beta(Y,w)=6$. Every
finite-dimensional realization with the prescribed exact pairwise
relations and every state satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantumtarget}
 \sum_{i=0}^{23}\ev{P_i}^2+\ev{P_6}^2+\ev{P_7}^2\leq6.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The binary adjacency matrix has
rank 14 over $\F_2$, and a seven-qubit Pauli realization is explicitly
checked for every pair. This is a local simulation, not a hardware run.

\begin{proof}
Put $d=128$ and label Bell coordinates by $v=(x,z)\in\F_2^{14}$.
Write $\tau(v)=(-1)^{x\cdot z}$ and let $[v,p]$ be the binary
symplectic form. For the archived Pauli labels $p_i$, define
\[
 c_v=\sum_i w_i\tau(p_i)(-1)^{[v,p_i]},\qquad
 A_{vu}=\frac{\tau(v+u)}{128}.
\]
The matrix $A$ is real symmetric and implements partial transposition
on Bell-diagonal states; its tensor-product and Walsh-transform
implementations are checked independently with integers.

Convexity permits maximizing the weighted squared expectations over
pure states. Twirling two identical copies by identical Pauli operators
preserves the objective and symmetric support, and produces a
Bell-diagonal probability vector $\lambda$ satisfying
\[
 \lambda\geq0,\quad \sum_v\lambda_v=1,\quad
 \lambda_v=0\ (\tau(v)=-1),\quad A\lambda\geq0.
\]
The last condition holds because the twirled state is separable. The
two-copy symmetric-support PPT relaxation is established prior art
\cite[Appendix B.2, Eqs.~(42)--(45)]{xsw2024}, not a new method here.

The archived rational vector $u$ has a common denominator of 332 bits.
Exact acceptance checks $u_v\geq0$ for all 16384 coordinates and
$(c+Au)_v\leq6$ for all 8256 coordinates with $\tau(v)=1$.
Thus, without a solver tolerance or rounding argument,
\[
 c^{\mathsf T}\lambda\leq(c+Au)^{\mathsf T}\lambda\leq6.
\]
The verifier regenerates $c$ from the archived operators, checks the
source hash, and evaluates $Au$ by two agreeing integer algorithms.
The common eigenstate of a maximum-weight stable set supplies the
matching lower bound six. Finally, weighted representation invariance
\cite[Theorems 10 and 12]{xsw2024} transfers this equality from the
archived realization to all realizations in the stated scope.
\end{proof}

The certificate is a computer-assisted proof for one graph and one
weight vector, not an all-weight or general SCF theorem. Discovery used
nearly active constraints only to select a square correction system;
those choices are not trusted by acceptance. Dixon lifting reconstructed
the rational correction at 40 lifts (640 modulus bits). Full-coordinate
checks, including constraints outside that system, prove the bound.
The portable certificate and standalone checker are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}.

\subsection{A bounded physical attack}
The registered attack fixes the amplitudes of its initial Hamiltonian
proposal from one first-moment relaxation and tests 1024 sign classes
modulo Pauli-conjugation gauge. The count is $2^{24-14}$; it is a finite
set of initializations, not an enumeration of continuous quantum states.
At each step it selects an extremal absolute-eigenvalue eigenvector of
$H(a)=\sum_i\sqrt{w_i}a_iP_i$ and updates $a_i$ proportional to
$\sqrt{w_i}\ev{P_i}$. The cap is 64 iterations per start and the
stopping tolerance is $10^{-10}$. The threshold for a numerical
violation candidate is $6+10^{-7}$.

All 1024 starts finished their allocated work. Only 144 met the
convergence test; 880 reached the iteration cap. The best saved state,
reevaluated with independent bitwise Pauli action, gives
$6.000000000000059$, consistent with six to floating-point precision.
The positive control on the archived eight-vertex $G_8$ of Xu et al.\
\cite[Fig.~11(a)]{xu} gives $3.0448154998549777>3$; it is labeled
\artifact{positive_G8} in the code.
The double-weight control, with weights two also at $17,18$, gives
$7.000000000000041$, consistent with seven. Its upper bound seven
already follows by summing two atom rows and the connector-edge bound.

One dense eigensolver failed after a saved 64-start prefix. A second
registered batch resumed without skipped classes; twelve resumed-start
calls used a checked SciPy fallback. The resumed batch took 214.594
seconds. The saved best and control states were reevaluated
independently, but all optimizer trajectories were not independently
replayed. These qualifications preclude a global-optimality claim.

\subsection{Exact weakness of the first-moment baseline}
Define the relaxation explicitly, with indices $0,\ldots,24$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
 \begin{aligned}
 \vartheta(Y,w)=\max_M\ &\sum_{i=0}^{23}w_iM_{i+1,i+1}\\
 \text{subject to }\ &M\succeq0,\quad M_{00}=1,\\
 &M_{0,i+1}=M_{i+1,i+1}\quad(0\leq i<24),\\
 &M_{i+1,j+1}=0\quad\text{for every edge }ij.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every physical squared profile is feasible in this relaxation. For a
pure state, use real Gram vectors $v_0=\psi$ and
$v_{i+1}=\ev{P_i}P_i\psi$: their real inner products obey the diagonal
equations, and the edge products have zero real part. Purification
extends this construction to mixed states.

\begin{proposition}[Rational relaxation witness]\label{prop:theta}
There exists a rational feasible matrix in \eqref{eq:theta} with
\[
 \sum_iw_iM_{i+1,i+1}=\frac{325328979}{50000000}=6.50657958.
\]
In particular, this relaxation cannot certify the bound six.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The archived matrix has common denominator $10^8$. The acceptance
algorithm checks symmetry and every affine equation exactly, and
computes a Fraction $LDL^{\mathsf T}$ factorization with 25 positive
diagonal pivots and exact reconstruction. The objective is the stated
rational number. This finite positive-definiteness certificate proves
feasibility without relying on the numerical solver's status.
\end{proof}
The witness proves a relaxation gap of $25328979/50000000$ above the
classical bound. It is \emph{not} a quantum state and does not refute
\eqref{eq:quantumtarget}. Conversely, the physical attack does not
give an upper bound that closes this gap; Theorem~\ref{thm:target}
closes it by a separate exact certificate.

The exact degree histogram of $Y$ is
$\{4:2,6:12,7:4,8:6\}$. In ordinary basic gear composition the two
unattached gear hubs have degree five \cite{gear}. Thus $Y$ is not a
direct output of that precise construction. General geared, fuzzy,
lifting and strip arguments are not excluded by this degree test.

\subsection{Classical ancestry and the remaining transfer question}
Deleting vertices $8,9,19,20$ leaves a twenty-vertex graph that does
admit two ordinary gear contractions. Exact reconstruction identifies
a nine-vertex edge-subdivided seed of stable bound three, a fourteen-
vertex intermediate of bound four, and the twenty-vertex core of bound
six. The first row transition is proper g-lifting; the second is proper
geared lifting with the left hubs doubled, in the terminology and
formulas of \cite[Definitions 2.4 and 2.7]{gear}. Reintroducing the four
deleted vertices in the displayed order gives maximal classical lifting
coefficient one each: every restricted stable optimum is five.
Therefore neither the classical row nor its construction is claimed new.
These classical operations do not themselves prove that squared quantum
expectations satisfy the lifted rows. Theorem~\ref{thm:target} settles
one final instance, without establishing a quantum transfer rule.

A preregistered small screen tested proper geared unit-seed rank rows
on 30 selected constructions from connected graphs of orders three
through six. There were 240 starts, capped at 32 steps each; none met
the strict convergence test. No violation above $10^{-6}$ was found.
Independent bitwise checks covered 31 saved best states including a
positive control of $3.0445923942>3$. This is weak falsification evidence,
not a global optimization result. It omits nonuniform seed facets,
proper g-lifting, and the four XX vertex additions.

\section{Verification, scope, and publication boundary}\label{sec:verification}
The manuscript draws on research snapshot \texttt{4dc9dc7}; the full
commit is in Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}, with later results through
C034 added separately. Discovery and acceptance
are separate programs. Universal algebra is checked by exact word
rewriting and integer amplitude polynomials. Polyhedral discovery uses
rational double description; independent acceptance clips every
halfspace from the full rational unit cube and verifies the final
vertices and facet ranks. Missing-facet and corrupted-certificate
controls must be rejected. Numerical states are checked independently
through their bitwise Pauli action.

The accepted clean-source reproduction used code/data commit
\texttt{a7a92f3}, followed by documentation-only commit
\texttt{4dc9dc7}. All 127 tests passed in one run in 441.989 seconds,
and all 64 artifact hashes matched. C011, C012, C014 and C016 discovery
outputs were regenerated; saved C015 states, not every optimization
trajectory, were reevaluated. These are same-host checks with different
algorithms, not external replication or proof-assistant verification.

For C031 separately, a clean scoped archive of commit \texttt{08b71d4}
passed the standalone standard-library exact verifier and eight C031
tests. The first archive attempt exposed a CRLF/LF source-hash mismatch;
explicit LF normalization fixed portability without changing any
coefficient. This later check did not rerun the historical 127-test
campaign or all discovery trajectories. Later C033 construction tests
and C034 state-audit tests are recorded separately.

Failed attempts remain in the ledger. A complete hull enumeration for
$Y$ exceeded a 300-second cap and is not accepted as complete. The
full-facet claim in Proposition~\ref{prop:target} instead uses exhaustive
stable-set enumeration for just the frozen weight. For $X^+$, two
clipping attempts timed out before acceptance; an exact candidate-edge
index eventually passed both the full hull and missing-facet checks.
The index retains the original exact adjacency rank test and falls back
to full pair scanning when its index budget is exceeded.

The established beta framework, closure rules and state-polynomial
relaxations are prior art \cite{xu}. XX-strips are published classical
objects \cite{cs}; gear composition describes classical stable-set
polytopes under specific hypotheses \cite{gear}. These facts must not
be relabeled as new quantum mechanisms. A quantum all-weight result for
the specified family is the candidate mathematical contribution, but
equivalence to a consequence of a larger known closure or embedding
remains a priority question. We do not infer novelty from a failed
keyword search. The wider source comparison and unresolved applicability
questions are retained in the repository's novelty audit. The line-graph
theorem is distinct: it covers a general free-fermion class and gives a
maximum-weight-matching oracle, but it does not imply the non-line SCF
results below.

The quantum consequences proved here are tight uncertainty bounds:
for every proved graph,
\[
 \sum_iw_i\operatorname{Var}_\rho(P_i)
 \geq \sum_iw_i-\alpha(G,w),
\]
since $P_i^2=I$. They also give valid Hamiltonian norm bounds by
\eqref{eq:variational} and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz. These are standard
consequences of all-weight equality, not demonstrated runtime or
sample-complexity advantages. No paid cloud experiment or QPU job was
performed. The certified composition facet is neither a hardware
advantage claim nor a completed universal SCF theorem.

\section{Conclusion}
Nuclear-norm duality and Edmonds' matching polytope prove that every line
graph is $\hbar$-perfect for all nonnegative weights. The result contains
an explicit infinite family outside the $h$-perfect sufficient class and
turns its weighted Pauli uncertainty problem into maximum-weight matching.
A coherent signed Gram representation and an exact fixed-core
refinement prove all-weight Pauli uncertainty for an explicit unbounded
SCF family. The same scalar envelope completes a three-row atom and
supplies two XX-based corollaries. The composed twenty-four-operator
target now has an exact rational quantum upper certificate matching
its classical bound six, despite a certified first-moment relaxation
gap. Its classical gear/lifting ancestry is established. The remaining
boundary for the broader SCF program is a conceptual quantum composition
theorem. External review and a broader priority assessment remain
necessary before a first-discovery or venue-level claim.
