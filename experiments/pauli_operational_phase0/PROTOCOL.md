# Operational Pauli-uncertainty experiments

## Research question and scope

This cycle changes the endpoint from an abstract all-weight theorem to an
operational numerical experiment: which physically realizable quantum states
allow a fixed Pauli squared-expectation inequality to be distinguished from
its stable-set bound after a specified channel and finite measurements?
The observables and bound are fixed before optimization. A violation is not
automatically an entanglement certificate; no separability bound is assumed.
These are local density-matrix/state-vector and measurement simulations,
not QPU data, circuit-depth demonstrations, or quantum runtime advantages.

The narrow empirical candidate is a transferable improvement in detection
under nonunital noise, after comparing against both noiseless optimization
and a noise-aware optimizer, giving every method the same oracle shot
allocation. Merely showing that the ideal optimum differs from the most
statistically significant point is NOT novel (Jungnitsch et al., 2010).
Merely reweighting observables for a Pauli-diagonal channel is also NOT novel:
the squared objective becomes an ordinary weighted beta problem exactly.
This latter channel is a reduction/control, not the main contribution.

The previous instruction to attempt an operator proof next is superseded
for this campaign by the explicit request to prioritize experimental work.
The existing C014-C016 data and mathematical statuses are unchanged.
The closed QAOA/MPS, scalar hardware-noise-witness and simple drift-curvature
claims remain closed; this cycle does not rebrand them.

## Frozen corpus and splits

Six observable systems are used:

1. Published G8, using the exact archived three-qubit words and weights.
2. Anti-C7, a standard binary Pauli realization with uniform weights.
3. Published G9, four-qubit words in Xu et al., equation (94), weights
   (1,1,1,1,1,1,1,2,2).
4. Anti-C9, a standard binary Pauli realization with uniform weights.
5. The proved C009 twelve-operator graph, weights one on its lights and two
   on its heavies: negative control for physical violations.
6. The open C014 twenty-four-operator, seven-qubit target: stress case, not
   a presumed negative control and not a novel graph-family sample.

G8 and anti-C7 are development families. G9 and anti-C9 are held-out
families. All hyperparameters and the runner are frozen before the first
held-out run. C009 is a correctness control, C014 an exploratory stress
case. Repeated states, Clifford frames and noise strengths do not increase
the number of independent graph families. The study has only two held-out
families; generalization is correspondingly limited.

For each graph use three Pauli frames: the original and two deterministic
Clifford-conjugated frames, each generated by 4q seeded H/S/CNOT gates.
Pairwise commutation and stable-set bounds must be identical in all frames.
All amplitudes are optimized freely, so any ideal frame dependence is
optimization error rather than a change of the ideal beta number.
Frames change local-channel action; they are not a new compilation method.

Noise settings are: none; independent local depolarization p=0.003,0.01;
independent local amplitude damping gamma=0.01,0.03. Depolarization uses
E(rho)=(1-p)rho+p I/2 on each qubit. Damping uses
K0=diag(1,sqrt(1-gamma)), K1=sqrt(gamma)|0><1|.
No identity-plus-reweight approximation is used for damping. Independent
density-matrix Kraus application checks the adjoint-operator computation.

Each graph/frame/noise cell has eight independent seeded repetitions.
Total: 6 x 3 x 5 x 8 = 720 paired cells and 2160 method jobs.
The schedule is round-robin over graph families, noise and frames inside
each repetition. Outcomes are written only after a complete paired cell;
unfinished cells are not compared. A three-hour cap can leave a prefix.

## Compared methods and resource accounting

- ideal_beta: see-saw maximization of the original noiseless squared
  expectation sum; select its best ideal state, then evaluate its noise.
- noisy_beta: see-saw maximization using the exact noise-dressed Hermitian
  observables; select the best noisy objective.
- noisy_score: normalized-state L-BFGS optimization of the smooth proxy
  (F-alpha)/(sum_i 2 w_i sqrt(mu_i^2(1-mu_i^2)+1e-12)+1e-8).
  The proxy is only for discovery, not a confidence bound. It starts from
  its own noise-aware see-saw seed within its own charged budget.

Every method gets at most three seconds of optimization in each cell and
at most 512 restarts; every local solve has an iteration/evaluation cap.
One numerical BLAS thread and one worker are used. Count elapsed time,
restarts, objective evaluations, convergence and stopping reason. Paired
methods rotate order across repetitions. The total campaign wall budget is
10800 seconds including setup and validation; a child cell is killed after
90 seconds or if it exceeds 2 GiB RSS. These are maximum budgets, not an
instruction to idle or manufacture work after the finite protocol completes.
No GPU/cloud/QPU use or model API calls are made by the runner.

All methods receive the SAME oracle allocation rule using their true noisy
expectations: n_i proportional to 2w_i sqrt(mu_i^2(1-mu_i^2)+1e-12), with
at least two shots per observable and exact accounting of the total shots.
This favorable oracle baseline isolates state selection from allocation.
It does not claim a deployable allocation without calibration or charge
unperformed pilot measurements. Preparation complexity and compiler cost
are outside phase 0, and must be added before a hardware-advantage claim.

## Independent validation and statistical endpoint

Freeze the selected state before validation. Apply the specified channel
with Kraus operators to its density matrix, and evaluate the original Pauli
words by a separately implemented bitwise action. Compare against adjoint
expectations with tolerance 1e-9; check trace, Hermiticity and positivity.
No comparison is accepted if this check fails.

Simulate independent Bernoulli Pauli measurements for total shot budgets
10000, 1000000 and 10000000, 256 independent simulated datasets each.
Compute marginal two-sided Clopper-Pearson intervals with Bonferroni
allocation of an overall delta=0.01/(720*3*3). Map each mean interval to
a lower bound on its square (zero if it crosses zero), and sum weighted
lower bounds. Detection means this LOWER bound exceeds the exact stable
bound. This is conservative simultaneous inference for the fixed finite
protocol, not a normal-approximation z-score or a time-uniform statement.
The 256 Monte Carlo repetitions estimate power; their intervals are not
256 opportunities to announce one successful experimental observation.

Report detection fractions with binomial uncertainty, noisy excess,
noiseless objective, expected allocation and wall time. The optimizer's
smoothed score is not a primary empirical result. Report cells with no
detectable effect and all timeouts; do not condition the headline result on
the best noisy frame or a successful seed. Comparisons aggregate within
graph family first, not by treating repeated cells as independent graphs.

## Controls, falsification, and gates

Prelaunch tests check Pauli bit ordering, Clifford symplectic preservation,
amplitude-damping/depolarizing adjoints against Kraus density evolution,
normalized-state analytic gradients against finite differences, exact
shot accounting, interval edge cases, corrupted-state rejection, and
positive G8 retrieval. C009 must never physically exceed three beyond
1e-8. A C014 excess above 1e-7 is a candidate requiring independent exact
follow-up; it does not become a theorem or a manuscript result automatically.

The default verdict is OPEN, not NOVEL. A promising empirical gate needs
an improvement against BOTH baselines in held-out nonunital cells, not
only versus noiseless optimization: at least 0.10 absolute increase in
mean detection power at a frozen budget in each held-out family, with
the remaining budgets and controls also reported. Selection among three
budgets must be disclosed and multiplicity addressed. This threshold
identifies a follow-up candidate, not a top-tier novelty certificate.

Kill the generic novelty claims already explained by prior work. Kill the
specific experimental candidate if the noise-aware beta baseline removes
the gain, the effect is confined to development families, only a smoothed
proxy improves, or exact channel/negative checks fail. Do not change
weights, split, noise or thresholds after seeing held-out results.
If the gate passes, the next separately registered experiment must add a
noise-model mismatch test and an independent measurement-allocation
baseline, then realistic state preparation and held-out hardware before
any claimed practical advantage. If it fails, retain the negative record
and choose a genuinely different quantum experimental capability.

## Reproduction and continuation

Source data are read only from the preceding research snapshot. Every
campaign stores its protocol hash, input hashes, code hashes, random seeds,
Python/package versions, per-cell artifacts and status. Writes are atomic;
an exclusive lock prevents duplicate campaigns. STOP stops at a safe
boundary. Existing outputs are never silently overwritten. The runner
does not self-launch another three-hour campaign. A scheduled research
review may read the completed campaign and preregister the next bounded
cycle, but must not bypass product limits or extend paid authority.
